# Rustic pellet stove and heating strategy

Date: 2026-10-25
Status: owner-approved design intent; manufacturer/model and technical design pending.

## Chosen heating concept: Option A
- Feature a **rustic, traditional-looking pellet stove** in the living-room hearth, with a separate independent whole-house heating system for backup and other zones.
- Favor a substantial cast-iron or cast-iron-look silhouette, traditional detailing, visible fire, and restrained black/dark iron finish. Avoid a conspicuously futuristic appearance.
- Architectural surround concept: traditional brick hearth, warm wood trim/mantel where permitted by listed clearances, optionally modest stone/brick details. Rustic aesthetics must not compromise service access.
- Preserve the welcoming, modest autumn palette and whimsical-grandma character.

## Design coordination
- Size stove only after room-by-room heat-loss calculations and evaluation of open-plan airflow and distribution to remote rooms.
- Confirm listed clearances to combustibles, hearth protection, exhaust route, outside combustion air where required, local code, and qualified installation.
- Coordinate thermostat/control strategy and avoid overheating the living room while other rooms remain cold.
- Provide safe dry pellet storage and convenient ash handling and maintenance access.
- Include electrical draw, backup power needs for controls/auger/fans, and outage behavior in technology/electrical planning.
- Do not imply the pellet stove alone is whole-house heat or will operate without electricity.
- Evaluate actual available models and service networks before specifying a product.

## Open choice
Select the exact stove and whether the surround leans more ornate cast-iron cottage or simpler farmhouse brick, after reviewing real product dimensions and installation manuals.
