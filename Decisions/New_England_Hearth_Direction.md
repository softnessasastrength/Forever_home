# New England hearth direction

Date: 2026-10-25
Status: owner-approved aesthetic direction; construction geometry and appliance remain unselected.

## Design decision
Split the difference between a late-19th-century farmhouse fireplace and an early-20th-century New England stove alcove. Traditional, modest, believable, warm, and not theatrical.

- Weathered red-brown clay brick, occasional darker variation, warm buff mortar.
- Very restrained, irregular limewash so brick color remains visible.
- Low broad herringbone brick hearth, subject to the selected pellet stove's listed floor protection requirements.
- Simple substantial oak mantel or restrained shelf **only where the selected appliance's combustible clearances allow**.
- Shallow brick chimney-breast visual composition, with traditional painted trim at either side.
- Rustic dark cast-iron-look pellet stove, visible flame, modest traditional detailing.
- Explore a shallow recessed alcove **only if the appliance listing, ventilation, service access, and combustible clearances explicitly permit it**.
- Avoid ornate Victorian carving, huge stone walls, faux-distressed brick, and glossy modern farmhouse styling.

## Construction cautions
This is a decorative masonry design direction, not approval of a structural masonry chimney. Compare real clay thin brick versus supported full-depth brick. Use the actual selected stove manual to establish hearth, venting, mantel, and alcove requirements. Verify floor loads, substrate, thermal protection, and local code.
