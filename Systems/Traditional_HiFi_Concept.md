# Traditional component hi-fi — Kenwood-inspired direction

Date: 2026-10-25
Status: owner-requested design direction; models, topology and procurement are unselected.

## Intent
A serious, visible, serviceable component hi-fi system with Kenwood-style late-1970s through 1990s character. Not a soundbar or hidden all-in-one wireless speaker system.

## Candidate components
- Stereo integrated amplifier or receiver (vintage Kenwood or comparable).
- Separate AM/FM tuner where appropriate.
- CD player/changer, cassette deck, optional turntable.
- Optional equalizer, selected based on actual signal chain.
- Proper passive floorstanding speakers, selected by listening tests, impedance/sensitivity and room acoustics.
- Dedicated furniture-grade ventilated oak component cabinet with accessible cabling and space for service.
- Source routing from DIRECTV/Mitsubishi rear-projection TV via appropriate conversion and input matching, without assuming HDMI or optical support on vintage hardware.

## Architecture and wiring
- Maintain stereo operation independent of TV, internet, automation and home-network services.
- Provide labeled speaker-wire routes and analog audio connections; conduit/spare pathways to permit future replacement.
- Plan dedicated power outlets, cable management, ventilation, speaker placement, room acoustics, and service access.
- Separate speaker placement from the rustic pellet stove's heat/clearance zone.
- Evaluate a stereo-first system vs a separate home-theater surround processor/receiver; do not presume vintage stereo amp decodes multichannel formats.

## Vintage equipment cautions
Inspect and service capacitors, switches, controls, protection circuits, tape belts, and turntable parts as needed; verify actual unit condition and safe electrical operation. Match amplifier and speaker loads; consider age, parts availability, and power draw.

## Open decisions
1. Silver-face 1970s/1980s vs black 1990s Kenwood aesthetic.
2. Stereo-first (2.0/2.1) vs dual-purpose hi-fi + discrete surround.
3. Primary sources: vinyl, cassette, CD, FM, DIRECTV, network/local music.
4. Speaker size and cabinet dimensions, after room layout and listening goals.
