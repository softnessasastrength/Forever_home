# Whole-build BOM and cost model — first-pass methodology

Status: conceptual only; no contractor quote or model takeoff.

## Scope
Include materials, quantities, waste, labor, equipment/rentals, subcontractors, permits, utilities, sitework, GC overhead, contingency, and commissioning. Track land acquisition and financing separately.

## Workbook fields
WBS code; level/zone; assembly; component; Revit family/type or source ID; specification; quantity; unit; waste factor; unit price; extended material cost; labor hours/rate; subcontract cost; equipment cost; source/date; confidence; assumption; inclusion/exclusion; revision.

## Scenarios
1. Owner-builder, with licensed trades and realistic self-performed scope.
2. GC-managed construction.
3. Turnkey complete and occupiable.

## Estimating progression
A. Program allowance (rough ranges, no false precision).
B. Model-derived quantity takeoff after dimensioned BIM.
C. Assembly-based consumables and allowances not automatically in BIM.
D. Location/site-adjusted vendor pricing and subcontract quotes.
E. Construction documents and procurement-ready BOM after engineering.

## Area accounting
Do not impose a 1,500 sf cap. Schedule lower/main/upper gross and conditioned areas, garage, porch, above/below grade, and unfinished zones separately. Reconcile actual footprints and floor elevations before calculating dollars per sf.

## Major WBS groups
Site and utilities; foundation and waterproofing; structural framing; roofing; siding/envelope; doors/windows; interior partitions; insulation/air sealing; drywall; finish carpentry; flooring/tile; cabinetry/counters; appliances; plumbing; electrical; HVAC/ventilation; low-voltage/DIRECTV; fire/life safety; garage/EVSE; porches/decks; landscaping; general conditions; design/engineering/permits; contingency.

## Cost integrity
- Never present conceptual ranges as supplier quotes.
- Record source and pricing date; use locality-specific pricing only when site is known.
- Avoid double-counting labor, GC overhead, and turnkey markups.
- Account for code-required systems, commissioning, waste, taxes, escalation, and contingency.
- Treat preliminary sketches and room dimensions as unverified until the model is coordinated.
