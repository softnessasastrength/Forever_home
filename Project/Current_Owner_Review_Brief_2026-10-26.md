# Current owner review brief

Date: 2026-10-26  
Status: working review packet; no geometry freeze or code-compliance claim

## What is established

- The project is a warm, collected, whimsical-grandma New England home with human-scale rooms and a modest exterior presence.
- The house must support togetherness and privacy, including the shared primary suite, Renn's boutique, and Branden's hybrid bedroom/office.
- The main level is organized around kitchen, dining, living, hearth, large cabinet rear-projection television, and a dining-connected three-season porch.
- The lower level is intended to be finished from the start and remain adaptable for in-law, guest, storage, laundry, mechanical, technology and future accessibility needs.
- Future accessibility, maintainable systems, replacement routes, solar/battery readiness, essential-load resilience, manual generator operation and provider-neutral low-voltage infrastructure are part of the design intent.
- Revit 2027 is authoritative. Existing pilot and Compact-Core files are studies, not an approved house model.

## What is not established

- Site, grade, jurisdiction, adopted code editions and final footprint.
- Final gross or conditioned area. The former 1,500 sf figure is not a cap.
- Selected schematic alternative. A and B remain comparisons; C is the next proposed native-Revit test.
- A continuous accessible route, stair/headroom solution, lift product, bath clearances or bedroom/egress qualification.
- Final hearth, television, solar/battery, generator, satellite, structural or MEP sizing.
- Revit 2027-to-2018 import and independent DWG fidelity.

## Current proposed test: Compact-Core C

C is a smaller B-derived study intended to reduce excess residual/core space while preserving the principal program. It is a test target, not a selected plan.

The test should verify, in native Revit:

1. shell, levels, floor assemblies and room boundaries;
2. native stairs, riser/tread geometry and headroom;
3. future lift reservation and a continuous route strategy;
4. kitchen/dining/living envelopes and the 8 x 4 ft island study;
5. primary, Jack & Jill and proposed main-bath privacy/clearance logic;
6. wet-stack and technology-area separation;
7. furniture, door, delivery and service routes; and
8. warnings, schedules and exports sufficient for an evidence-backed review note.

## Owner decisions still needed after the test

- Select C, retain B, compare against A, or reopen the alternatives.
- Accept, revise or reject the proposed main full bath.
- Choose the preferred accessibility strategy: lift-dependent route, main-level sleeping/bathing provision, or another documented approach.
- Confirm whether the current area allowance is emotionally and financially acceptable once room, circulation and service areas are reconciled.
- Confirm unresolved equipment preferences only after actual dimensions, clearances and service paths are documented.

## Review boundary

This packet supports design coordination only. It is not a permit set, stamped design, engineering calculation, code certification or construction approval. Safety-critical open issues remain blockers to declaring design review complete.

## Review sequence

Use this packet in the following order:

1. Confirm the vision-preservation charter and room-program intent.
2. Review the current owner decisions and unresolved tradeoffs below.
3. Inspect the Compact-Core C evidence and checklist.
4. Record owner choices in the coordination register before changing geometry.
5. Only then authorize another native-Revit test or an alternative comparison.

The sequence is deliberately reversible: reviewing C does not select C, replace A/B, or authorize a geometry freeze.
