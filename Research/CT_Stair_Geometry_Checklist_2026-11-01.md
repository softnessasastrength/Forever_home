# Connecticut residential stair geometry screen — 2026-11-01

Status: **source-based screening only; occupancy, permit path, authority interpretation, and actual stair geometry remain unresolved.** This note complements, and does not alter, the historical source-bound note `Connecticut_Stair_Code_Research_2026-10-30.md`.

## Current source basis

Connecticut DAS lists the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code (CSBC) as current and says it applies to permit applications filed from October 1, 2022. It adopts the 2021 IRC with Connecticut amendments. The exact municipality, permit date, occupancy classification, and any applicable exceptions are not established for this study. Confirm them with the local building official and the project's licensed design team.

The state code's amended tread minimum is 9 in; the owner's house specification prefers 10½ in and states a 10 in design minimum. The more conservative owner preference remains a separate design target; it does not change the adopted-code text.

## Section mismatch carried forward

The selected-D test levels remain −5, 0, and +5 ft, a 60 in adjacent-level difference. The owner's separate DD-013 study direction prefers six risers × 7¼ in = 43½ in. That leaves 16½ in between the preferred stair rise and a 60 in selected-D level change. Neither the source levels nor the 43½ in preference is silently adjusted here. A full inter-level stair cannot be accepted from this partial arithmetic; the section/landing arrangement must be resolved before native stair placement.

M289's additional 36 in route proxy is an analysis precedent, not a stair-width rule or adopted accessibility dimension. M289 remains analysis-only and no candidate is selected.
