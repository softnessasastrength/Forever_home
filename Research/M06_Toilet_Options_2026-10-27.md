## What “industrial” can mean here

There are three materially different choices. A strong residential gravity toilet remains a conventional tank toilet. A pressure-assisted residential toilet uses a sealed vessel inside a tank and must use a bowl designed for it. A true commercial flushometer toilet is a bowl plus a flushometer valve, with much higher instantaneous flow demands. They are not interchangeable merely because the product pages all promise a mighty flush.

## Preliminary recommendation

The owner selected the **Champion PRO** as the first toilet candidate to investigate, specifically the currently listed 610AA001.020. Include the **Flushmate 503H** only as a pressure-assist comparison if its distinctly different operation is appealing. Do not start with a true flushometer bowl: its specified 25 gpm flowing demand is a substantial unresolved house-service input. If the owner wants the full commercial flushometer experience despite that, test it explicitly as an alternate and require a plumbing design check before carrying it forward. The latest manufacturer's low-pressure-performance claim does not remove its stated minimum fixture supply of 25 psi flowing at 25 gpm.

### KOHLER Highcliff Ultra: current source and system dependencies (2026-10-28)

The current [K-96057 US/Canada product sheet](https://techcomm.kohler.com/techcomm/pdf/K-96057_spec_US-CA_Kohler_en.pdf), dated 2026-10-27, identifies the product as a **flushometer bowl**, not a complete toilet kit. Its nominal diagram gives a 26-1/8 in front-to-back body length, 14-5/8 in width, and 16-5/8 in rim height; the technical section gives 12 in standard rough-in while the feature list and drawing allow 10 or 12 in. KOHLER's current product page describes the rim as 16-7/8 in, a 1/4-in discrepancy to resolve with the installation drawing before vertical/elevation use. The sheet also lists a 2-1/8 in trapway, 10 × 7 in water surface and 2,500 lb static-load claim; none of those statements is a room-fit or structural approval.

The same current sheet lists a **minimum flowing supply of 25 psi at 25 gpm** and offers K-76320 (1.1 gpf), K-76321 (1.28 gpf) and K-76322 (1.6 gpf) manual flushometers. The flushometer and bowl form a matched system; the bowl page cautions that the valve's rated flush volume must match the bowl's WaterSense counterpart for the complete system's performance claim. KOHLER also says the bowl is engineered to flush at low pressure/flow, while the explicit minimum fixture flow remains 25 gpm. Treat those as system design requirements, not a contradiction that can be settled from marketing language. Confirm dynamic pressure/flow at the fixture, valve model and supply arrangement with a qualified plumbing designer; no project service capacity has been provided.

The official [KOHLER K-96057 page](https://www.kohler.com/en/products/commercial/shop-commercial-toilets/highcliff-ultra-floor-mounted-top-spud-flushometer-bowl-96057) identifies the product and separate bowl role. The current sheet's nominal height is 16-5/8 in, while the product page says 16-7/8 in to rim; the older 2024 [manufacturer sheet](https://resources.kohler.com/webassets/kpna/catalog/pdf/en/K-96057_spec_US-CA_Kohler_en.pdf) also lists 16-5/8 in and a 35–80 psi static-pressure range. The newly dated 2026 sheet does not repeat that static-pressure range, so do not carry the older range forward as current without confirmation. KOHLER's [K-76321 manual 1.28 gpf valve page](https://www.kohler.com/en/products/commercial/shop-commercial-flushometers/primme-manual-toilet-flushometer-1-28-gpf-76321-76321) is a candidate pairing, not a selected component. ## Safety and evidence cautions

- Flushmate's recall applies to specified Flushmate III Series 503 vessels manufactured from October 14, 1997 through April 30, 2011, because a vessel could rupture. It does **not** establish that a newly specified 503H toilet is affected; it does mean a used/legacy pressure-assist unit must have its exact model and serial checked against the current [Flushmate recall notice](https://www.flushmate.com/recall).
- Do not retrofit a pressure vessel into a gravity toilet. Use only the toilet models listed as compatible by the pressure-assist-system manufacturer.
- Do not treat “ADA compliant” product marketing as a room-layout determination. Manufacturer sheets themselves qualify such statements by the specific installation requirements.
- Product sheets are evidence of nominal product geometry and the manufacturer's stated requirements, not independent performance rankings, local code review, or a plumbing design.
