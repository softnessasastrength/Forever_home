## Roof snow-load notes

The adopted CSBC contains both an IRC amendment for residential buildings and IBC amendments. The eventual code path depends on building classification and jurisdiction; the IBC provisions below are not applied here as though they automatically govern this house.

- Connecticut's 2022 amendment to IRC R301.6 directs roof design to the snow load in Table R301.2(1). A steep profile does not itself establish a lower design load.
- In the IBC amendments, §1603.1.3 requires ground snow-load data and, where the listed threshold is exceeded, additional roof snow-load and drift information. Section 1608.1.2 sends sloped-roof snow-load calculations to ASCE 7 §7.4; the amendment sets a 30 psf minimum for the flat-roof snow load used in that calculation except where the code permits otherwise, and restricts use of the “unobstructed slippery roofs” reduction unless the building official approves it.
- The CSBC's IRC amendment for existing structures lists a roof change to a steeper pitch that raises the roof relative to the eave as a condition requiring reinforcement to the new-structure wind-loading requirements. That is an existing-building alteration provision; it is not being treated as the rule for this new-house concept.

These are prompts for the eventual site-specific professional design. They do not provide a pitch, framing system, member size, connection, snow load, wind load or approval for this model.

## Upper-suite sloped-ceiling screen — 2026-10-30

The Connecticut DAS-hosted **2022 CSBC with Errata #1**, Amendments to the 2021 IRC, §R305.1, states 7 ft minimum ceiling height for habitable spaces and hallways, and 6 ft 8 in for bathrooms, toilet rooms and laundry rooms. Its sloped-ceiling exception says the required floor area of the room must have at least 5 ft ceiling height, and at least 50% of the required floor area must have at least 7 ft ceiling height. A further bathroom/toilet exception requires usable height above fixtures; a shower or tub with showerhead requires 6 ft 8 in over at least a 30 × 30 in area at the showerhead. The detailed application of “required floor area,” room classification and exceptions belongs to the applicable code review, not this schematic screen.

This rule cannot be compared directly to M101's roof-plane heights: M101 measures ideal roof surface relative to an unassigned eave, while R305.1 concerns finished ceiling height above finished floor. The missing conversion inputs include the floor/eave elevation difference, wall/eave location, roof depth and ceiling finish/build-down. Fixture positions are also not fixed. No M101 option is screened as passing or failing R305.1.
