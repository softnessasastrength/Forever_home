# M22 pellet-stove envelope research — 2026-10-28

Status: **source-backed candidate only; no appliance or hearth wall selected.** This research supports the approved visual direction for a modest traditional pellet hearth, but it does not establish a product choice or an installation design.

## Candidate for gross-envelope screening

The current Harman P-Series manufacturer manuals include the **P43-C** freestanding pellet stove. The 2025 installation manual lists actual reference dimensions of 22 in wide × 28-5/16 in deep × 33-1/2 in high, a 3 in vent, 50 lb hopper and wood-pellet fuel. The 2025 owner manual identifies the P43-C model and describes the P-Series appliance as hand-built cast iron. This is a useful candidate for testing the owner's rustic, visible-flame pellet-stove direction; it is **not selected**, and exact finish, local availability and an exact Revit family remain unverified.

The manufacturer clearance information is recorded in [`M22_Pellet_Stove_Envelope_2026-10-28.json`](M22_Pellet_Stove_Envelope_2026-10-28.json). The current P-Series installation manual shows a 2 in rear clearance to combustible walls; side clearance of 10 in with side shields or 16 in without; a 9 in corner clearance; and a diagrammed 36 in front combustible separation. It requires non-combustible floor protection; its P43-C rectangular minimum is 25-7/16 × 26-3/4 in, with the manual's specified 6 in extensions measured from the identified appliance datums. Those are manufacturer minimums, not service, circulation or design clearances. The manual cautions that the minimum wall clearances leave little room for cleaning and service.

The manuals do not provide a mantel clearance or a P43-C alcove/recess assembly. The word “alcove” appears only in vent-termination guidance, which says to avoid venting into alcove locations; it does not approve enclosing the stove in an architectural recess. Therefore the approved shallow-breast / optional recess aesthetic cannot yet be converted into construction geometry. The selected manual must govern the actual appliance, wall construction and finishing details.

The owner-selected D room-space study keeps M-01 at 16 × 15 ft (240 sf), 20 sf above the preliminary 180–220 sf room-program range. The current main-floor blocks show one 16 ft edge facing the dining block across its nominal 6 in separation, and the opposite 16 ft edge with no adjacent room block. The two 15 ft edges relate to M-05 arrival and the remaining main-floor test-shell side. These are room-block relationships, not wall faces or compass directions.

The accompanying M22 comparison tests **two unselected arrangements**: put the hearth on one 16 ft edge and the rear-projection TV on the opposite edge, then swap them. Both leave the room's M-05-facing edge out of the focal-wall proposal and keep the hearth and media zones on separate walls. It is a relationship sketch only: it places neither product, assumes no door/window-free wall, and does not prove viewing distance, hearth clearance, sightline, circulation, venting or egress. The legacy Mitsubishi WS-65815 remains a visual reference; the owner has not selected an exact TV or size, so no TV placeholder is dimensioned or placed.
